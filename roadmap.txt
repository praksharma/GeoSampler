## Proposed README.md

# GeoSampler

**Geometry-aware point sampling for scientific machine learning**

GeoSampler is an open-source desktop application for generating spatial and parametric point datasets from 2D and 3D geometries.

It provides an interactive graphical interface for importing CAD geometries, identifying and labelling boundaries, configuring sampling strategies, refining point distributions, and exporting structured datasets.

GeoSampler is designed primarily for **Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs)**, but it is independent of any particular machine-learning framework, governing equation, or numerical solver.

## Motivation

Generating training points for PINNs is straightforward in simple rectangular domains but becomes increasingly complicated when working with realistic engineering geometries.

Curved boundaries, internal holes, complex surfaces, and non-uniform sampling requirements often demand custom geometry-specific scripts.

GeoSampler aims to eliminate much of this repetitive work through a reusable, interactive point-generation environment.

## Core Features

### 1. Geometry Import and Visualisation

- Support for 2D and 3D geometries.
- Import common CAD and geometry formats, including STEP and STL.
- Interactive geometry visualisation.
- Select individual faces, edges, and vertices.
- Highlight, isolate, and hide selected geometric regions.
- Support geometries containing holes and multiple boundary regions.

### 2. User-Defined Boundary Regions

- Interactively select one or more faces or edges.
- Assign arbitrary user-defined labels.
- Group multiple geometric entities under a single label.
- No predefined boundary-condition types or physics assumptions.
- Allow regions to remain unlabelled.
- Independently configure sampling for labelled and unlabelled regions.

GeoSampler treats boundary labels as metadata. It does not interpret or enforce physical boundary conditions.

### 3. Sampling Methods

Planned sampling strategies include:

- Uniform random sampling.
- Regular grid sampling.
- Latin Hypercube Sampling (LHS).
- Sobol sequences.
- Halton sequences.
- Hammersley sequences.
- Stratified sampling.
- Other extensible sampling strategies.

Sampling will support both domain interiors and geometric boundaries, with appropriate geometric constraints.

### 4. Sampling Configuration and Refinement

- Configure sampling independently for each region.
- Specify the number of sample points.
- Apply global or region-specific sampling strategies.
- Increase sampling density around selected locations.
- Refine near corners, edges, and geometric features.
- Support distance-based and user-defined spatial sampling densities.
- Control random seeds for reproducibility.
- Preview sampling distributions before export.

### 5. Geometry Parametrisation

Define families of related geometries through geometric parameters.

Potential capabilities include:

- Parametric lengths, widths, radii, and thicknesses.
- Moving or rotating geometric features.
- Parameter-dependent boundary positions.
- Multiple geometric parameters.
- Cartesian-product parameter combinations.
- LHS and quasi-random sampling of parameter spaces.
- Automatic point generation across geometry configurations.
- Parameter-dependent boundary identification.

#### Parametric Visualisation

Instead of directly visualising high-dimensional geometries, GeoSampler will provide:

- Interactive parameter sliders.
- Arrows indicating geometric movement.
- Minimum and maximum geometry overlays.
- Highlighting of parametrised features.
- Interactive previews of selected parameter configurations.

### 6. Dataset Export

Primary export formats:

- **xarray:** Structured, labelled multidimensional datasets.
- **NumPy NPZ:** Dictionary-based compressed arrays.

Exported data may include:

- Spatial coordinates.
- Geometric parameter values.
- Boundary and region labels.
- Surface normal vectors where applicable.
- Sampling configurations and metadata.
- Random seeds for reproducibility.

GeoSampler does not calculate PDE solutions or prescribe boundary values. Solution data, if required, must be obtained independently.

### 7. Project Management

- Save and reopen sampling projects.
- Preserve geometry references and region definitions.
- Store sampling strategies and refinement configurations.
- Regenerate datasets using saved settings.
- Support reproducible research workflows.

## Design Philosophy

**Geometry first.** Sampling should be driven by actual geometry rather than hard-coded coordinate constraints.

**Physics agnostic.** The software does not impose governing equations, boundary conditions, or solution methods.

**User controlled.** Researchers decide which regions to sample, how to name them, and how points should be distributed.

**Reproducible.** Sampling configurations and random seeds should be preserved.

**Framework independent.** Exported datasets should be usable with PyTorch, JAX, TensorFlow, NumPy, or other scientific computing tools.

**Open and extensible.** Sampling algorithms and geometry-processing components should remain modular and reusable.

One thing I would particularly emphasise during development is geometric correctness. A beautiful distribution of points is worthless if some lie outside the domain, surface sampling is biased unintentionally, or region labels are assigned incorrectly.

I'd make numerical and geometric validation a release requirement rather than an optional feature.
